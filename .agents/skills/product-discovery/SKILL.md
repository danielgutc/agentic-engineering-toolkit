# Product Discovery

Turn an opportunity into evidence-based product direction without prematurely selecting a solution.

## Workflow

1. State the problem, affected users or actors, current behavior, and desired outcome.
2. Separate observed evidence from stakeholder beliefs, assumptions, and unanswered questions.
3. Describe the important user journeys, contexts, pain points, accessibility needs, and experience risks.
4. Use design-thinking divergence to explore alternative ways to achieve the outcome before converging on a product direction.
5. Define the MVP as the smallest coherent outcome that creates value and tests the highest-risk assumption.
6. Record goals, non-goals, assumptions, hypotheses, and measurable success signals.
7. Prepare a product handoff that is sufficient for requirements work and identify decisions requiring approval.

## Boundaries

- Do not define architecture, technologies, APIs, containers, components, or implementation tasks.
- Do not treat the MVP as a low-quality version or as an arbitrary list of features.
- Keep user outcomes distinct from requested solutions and stakeholder preferences.
- Mark uncertain claims explicitly and recommend research rather than inventing evidence.
